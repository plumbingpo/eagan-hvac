Every time your thermostat calls for heat, the furnace works through a fixed checklist. The inducer motor starts and proves draft by closing a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter heats up, the gas valve opens, the burners light, and the flame sensor confirms there is fire. Only after all of that does the blower begin sending warm air into the house. Finding the step that fails is most of the job. Around Eagan, MN, the usual failure points are a flame sensor fouled with oxide, an igniter cracked by years of heating and cooling, a pressure switch that stays open because of a weak inducer or a clogged condensate trap on a high-efficiency unit, and control boards with burned relays. Each one halts the startup, and from the hallway they all look alike.

Rather than guess, Eagan HVAC tests the sequence one stage at a time. We read igniter resistance, measure flame signal in microamps, check that the pressure switch responds to real inducer draft, confirm voltage at the gas valve, and test the blower and its capacitor. Because this is a fuel-burning appliance, the safety basics come with every visit: we inspect the heat exchanger, leak-test the gas connections, and verify flue draft. You cannot tell a furnace problem from a furnace hazard by looking at the thermostat. Before we leave, temperature rise is measured against the data plate so the fire and the airflow are balanced as the manufacturer intended.

We work on gas, propane, and electric models from every major brand, both conventional 80 percent units and condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drains, and dual pressure switches. The truck stock covers the parts that actually fail: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most repairs finish in one trip. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Eagan

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Replacing the switch when the real issue is airflow removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

Our experienced technicians look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve goes unnoticed. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, catching airflow problems before they cut years off the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. What you get is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
