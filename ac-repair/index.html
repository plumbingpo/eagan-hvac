An outside unit that hums while the fan stays still is the call we get most in July in Eagan, MN, and the cause is usually a failed run capacitor. That inexpensive component starts the compressor and fan, fades for years without complaint, and then quits under peak load. It is far from the only suspect, though. Contactors pit and stop passing clean power, condenser fan bearings dry out, small refrigerant leaks drain away cooling capacity, and evaporator coils ice over when a clogged filter strangles airflow.

Warm air from the vents can mean a failed capacitor, a refrigerant leak, a frozen coil, or a duct problem, and each one comes with a very different price to fix. That is why Eagan HVAC tests before touching anything. We meter capacitors against their microfarad rating, compare compressor and fan amp draws to the data plate, judge the refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling instead of pressure alone, and inspect the coil, blower wheel, and filter whenever there is ice. Adding refrigerant without locating the leak only buys cold air until the charge leaks out again.

We service every common residential cooling system from all major brands, and our trucks stock the summer failure parts: capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, so cold air usually returns before we leave the driveway. When a compressor has truly failed on an aging system, we say so plainly and lay out the repair-versus-replace math honestly. The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

An air conditioner puts several hazards into one cabinet. The capacitor can deliver a painful shock long after the breaker is off, the condensing unit is wired for 240 volts, and the refrigerant circuit is a sealed, pressurized loop that federal rules forbid venting. Servicing it properly takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a DIY recharge can. Diagnosis has its own traps. A frozen coil is as likely to be an airflow issue as a charge issue, and recharging a system that still leaks throws money away while moisture and acid work on the compressor.

Professional repair gets the whole picture. Our experienced technicians meter electrical components instead of swapping them, weigh charge decisions against measured superheat and subcooling, find leaks with detectors and dye rather than assumptions, and check the filter, blower, and duct static pressure whenever airflow is suspect. That is the difference between a repair that holds through the season and a parts-cannon fix that calls you back in three weeks. And when the system genuinely is not worth saving, we tell you that plainly too, with numbers instead of pressure.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
  • Hose off the condenser coil every spring and trim plants to at least two feet away
  • A new buzz or click at startup often means a weak capacitor, so call before it fails
  • Flush the condensate drain line with the season's first use to head off clogs
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Have the AC serviced every spring, ahead of the first real heat
  • Replace the filter every month in summer, since restricted airflow is the top cause of frozen coils
